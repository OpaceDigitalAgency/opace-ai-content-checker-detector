## Trained model, deployment and rules demotion (cycle 2, 28 August 2026)

This section is the newest state of the programme. It supersedes any earlier accuracy figure recorded in this folder.

**What ships.** `tier3-cycle2-e5small-int8-perchannel.onnx` — intfloat/e5-small, 33.36M parameters, exported to per-channel int8, 34.3 MB, served from the site over HTTP 200 and executed in the visitor's browser. The full training record is `implementation/services/local-engine/research/cycle2-train/CYCLE2-REPORT.md`.

**Headline accuracy, browser-measured.** At the shipped rule — the minimum-evidence pair, primary 0.9855 or second-highest 0.9763, under segments-v3 — through onnxruntime-web (the runtime that actually ships), over the whole 5,558-document fresh long-form corpus (922 AI from 13 current models and 4,636 human from Europe PMC, GOV.UK, CRS, Global Voices, Mongabay, SEC EDGAR and PERSUADE): **889/922 (96.4%) AI detection at 90/4,636 (1.94%) human false positives**. The EU server route at the same pair reads 883/922 (95.8%) and 45/4,636 (0.97%). The corpus is not fully independent of training: of the 922 AI documents 654 are independent of every cycle-2 split and 268 are not, 168 of them in the training split itself; the human half is effectively independent at 11 of 4,636. This line previously named 0.984 as the live operating point and reported 90.3% detection at 1.34% false positives, and described the corpus as documents the model had never seen; 0.984 is retired, and both of those readings are withdrawn. Source: `measured.headline` and the `0.9855/0.9763` rows of `public/models/local-signals-v1/thresholds.json`.

**Why the threshold was refitted through the browser.** Python onnxruntime and onnxruntime-web disagree by a median 0.113 on this quantised model, because Python applies extended int8 fusions the web build does not. Shipping a Python-fitted threshold would have produced materially higher real-world false positives than the interface claimed, so the threshold was refitted through the shipped runtime and every published number is browser- or server-measured at the point it names. That refit first produced the 0.984 single-threshold rule, which is now itself retired; the rule that ships is the minimum-evidence pair, primary 0.9855 or second-highest 0.9763, under segments-v3. The 0.980 and 0.984 comparison figures this line used to carry describe retired rules, and are kept as such in `thresholds.json` under `server_fp32_segmented.by_threshold`.

**Against the previous model.** On 6,183 held-out cycle2-train rows: AUROC 0.530 to 0.9695; detection at a 1% false-positive budget 6.7% to 76.9%; at 2% 9.1% to 81.2%.

**Per register**, on the fresh 5,558-document corpus **at the retired 0.984 single-threshold rule, under segments-v2** — not at the shipped pair, and no row here may be placed beside a shipped-pair figure without naming the rule it came from: company updates 99/99 (100.0%), white papers 102/103 (99.0%), long-form journalism 132/137 (96.4%), research summaries 112/117 (95.7%), stories 108/114 (94.7%), academic discussion 107/113 (94.7%), academic literature reviews 100/107 (93.5%), academic essays 117/132 (88.6%). These sum to that retired rule's own 877/922, not to the headline above. The shipped-pair breakdown is Table 3 of [`../measurements/DETECTION-BY-LENGTH-AND-MODEL.md`](../measurements/DETECTION-BY-LENGTH-AND-MODEL.md). The denominator-free percentages this line carried previously were a third, older set again and are superseded. Every long-form category clears the 50% floor set in `OBJECTIVE.md` with margin.

**Edited AI.** An AI draft that a person then tidies is detected 82.3% of the time. An AI rewrite of a human original (HAT v6-v8) is detected 30-35% of the time. Human text that a language model polished is deliberately not flagged: a median 93.5% of those words are the human author's, and flagging it would accuse writers who use a model on their own prose. The earlier live copy saying lightly-edited AI is "missed almost entirely" was wrong, understated the tool, and was corrected in commit `ce56ac54`. It must not be restored.

**Short text.** Binned by the words a passage actually has, and scored at the shipped pair through the fp32 reference runtime: 29 of 172 AI passages of 100 to 199 words are detected (16.9%), against 193 of 228 at 300 to 399 (84.6%). Short human text is not falsely flagged at either length — 0/388 under 100 words and 0/732 at 100 to 199. The bands under 100 words and 200 to 299 hold fewer than 30 AI passages each, so no detection rate is published for either. Treat a draft under about 300 words as unreliable. The figures this line carried until 30 August 2026 — 67% at 200 words, 50% at 150 and 19% at 100 — are withdrawn, because they were scored at the retired 0.980 single threshold, recorded no per-length AI denominator, and were never re-measured on a shipping runtime. Source: `measured.length_sensitivity` in `thresholds.json`.

**The 113 writing rules are demoted.** The engine runs 116 named rules: 113 weighted writing-signal categories (`en-signals:2026.08.6`) plus three `en-gb:2026.08.1` rules. Of the 116, **95 fire on real documents**; `tier3-phrase-cluster` cannot fire on realistic prose and 20 more are dormant (`docs/CAPABILITIES.md` §3.4a). On the same 5,558 fresh documents they detected 45.1% of AI writing while flagging 24.8% of human writing — worse than the model on both axes at once. Binding decision: the rules tier no longer contributes to the AI verdict at all. It is presented as "Writing suggestions", feedback on phrasing and structure, never evidence of authorship. Any AI reading comes from the model alone. The `finding_breadth` escalation message claiming human controls peaked at two categories is falsified — real humans reach five, six and once eleven — and it caused 135 of 139 rules-layer false positives.

**The verdict is now three independent axes, never merged** (`implementation/packages/core/src/verdict/combine.ts`, 2026.08.8): `ai_probability`, which only a trained model may set and which reads `not_assessed` when no model is present (and `not_assessed` does not mean human); `text_integrity` (`clean` / `attention` / `manipulated`) for invisible carriers, homoglyphs, private-use clusters and watermark marks, which reports manipulation of the text and never says "this is AI"; and `editorial` (`none` / `some` / `many`) from the writing rules. The old single `classification` field and its `ai_like` escalations are gone. A hidden zero-width character proves text manipulation, not AI origin.

**Cycle 3 was built, measured and not shipped.** It improves AI-rewrites-of-human from 30% to 46-56%, but int8 quantisation costs it 5.2 points of recall so it cannot run in the browser at all, stories regress from 79.8% to 69.3% and journalism from 89.1% to 81.0%. Window scoring does not transfer to the deployed model: tested on cycle 2, 120-word window aggregation collapses detection from 86.4% to 1.8%, verified rather than assumed.

**Live production test through the real page**, model enabled: the owner's ChatGPT article 98.9%, Gemini 3.5-Flash article 98.7%, Claude Sonnet 5 article 98.4% (on the threshold), all flagged; a human office memo control 64.9%, not flagged. All three articles previously scored 6/100 and read as "No strong AI-style signals".

**Signal science** (`implementation/services/local-engine/research/signal-science/SIGNAL-SCIENCE.md`), on a 25,723-document corpus with 122 interpretable features and 5,935 matched pairs: repetition dominates and machine prose under-repeats (adjacent-sentence content-word overlap AUROC 0.912; MATTR 0.911; median machine document repeats 2.1% of content words between neighbouring sentences against 6.3% for human). Sentence-length burstiness, the most-cited heuristic in the field, scores AUROC 0.521 and is worthless. "AI vocabulary" scores 0.578. Causal ablation on the deployed artefact (400 AI + 400 human, 0.984 threshold, baseline 90.0%): making the text repeat itself more costs 33.0 points; deleting every AI-vocabulary word and phrase costs 0.8. A transparent 24-feature scorecard reaches 72.1% detection at a 1% false-positive budget against the neural model's 89.8% on the same held-out data (793 machine / 4,179 human), so transparency costs 17.7 percentage points.
