Seeing them side-by-side changes my assessment quite a bit. I was too generous with the live version at 8/10. I was judging it partly as a live, finished website rather than against what the interface could become.
Against these mockups, I would put the current expanded live version at about 7.0/10.

1. content-detector-mockup4(3).png — 9.2/10
This is comfortably my favourite.
It solves the biggest weakness in the live version: I instantly understand the result and can simultaneously see the evidence that produced it.
The two-column arrangement is excellent:
Your draft → Your result
The eye naturally goes to:
Moderate AI-pattern match
Then the explanation, then the strength indicator, then the two secondary findings:
Text integrity — Clean
Writing notes — 4 suggestions
That's nearly exactly the hierarchy this product needs.
The highlighted text on the left is especially strong. It makes the detector feel useful rather than merely judgemental. I'm not just being told that something happened; I can see where.
And importantly, it doesn't look like an analytics dashboard.
There are still things I'd change. I'd hide 0.9912 from the default view, because that's already drifting into technical-detail territory. I'd also soften the meter slightly so it doesn't accidentally imply scientific precision.
But structurally this is excellent.
This is the design I would build from.
2. content-detector-mockup2(3).png — 8.8/10
Very close.
The upper section is arguably even calmer than mockup4. The left-hand input and right-hand conclusion work extremely well, and:
Moderate AI-pattern match
is much stronger UX than the live version's smaller:
AI PATTERNS — No signal
It feels like a product answering a question rather than a diagnostic panel reporting telemetry.
Where it falls behind mockup4 is lower down.
You get:
Where the pattern appears
followed by:
Your draft — analysed
which essentially presents the same evidence again.
That makes the experience longer than it needs to be.
I'd steal quite a lot from this version, but use mockup4's approach of making the draft itself interactive rather than reproducing it again below.
The 6 checks included row is useful, although I would probably avoid making Customise prominent. Your original brief was right: users shouldn't feel they need to configure the machinery before trusting it.
3. content-detector-mockup3(3).png — 8.4/10
This is a different beast.
As a vision for the complete product, I'd actually score it about 9.3/10.
The four screens make the proposition very clear:
Text → URL → website → media
and the website scan in particular is far ahead of what most detector interfaces offer.
The line:
42 pages checked · 5 need attention · 2 contain strong AI-writing signals · 1 has verified AI-origin evidence · 3 have source or citation issues
is exactly how a website owner wants this information presented.
Likewise, the media result correctly distinguishes:
Verified provenance
from:
Synthetic-media detection
That's strategically important.
But as a visual direction for Opace, I wouldn't choose it over mockup4. The navy application sidebar and overall structure make it feel more like a generic enterprise SaaS product. You lose some of the personality of the existing Opace site.
So this is where I'd take the information architecture and future features, rather than the complete visual language.
4. content-detector-mockup1(3).png — 7.7/10
This one is actually very good UX thinking underneath a fairly plain visual treatment.
Its biggest achievement is reduction.
You get:
Some AI signal
followed immediately by:
Two of the three sections strongly match patterns our AI checker has learned.
That's remarkably easy to understand.
The responsive versions are also useful because they demonstrate that the concept genuinely survives down to mobile rather than simply shrinking a desktop interface.
But it feels like a wireframe/design-system proposal rather than a finished premium product.
There isn't enough visual hierarchy between important evidence and ordinary interface controls, and the branding has been reduced almost too far.
I'd borrow its restraint, not its appearance.
Current live version — revised to 7.0/10
The comparison makes its weaknesses much clearer.
Your live version isn't ugly. Quite the opposite: the surrounding Opace website is the most characterful of all of them.
The hero:
Free AI Content Integrity Checker
looks very good.
The orange, cream and black identity also has much more personality than the predominantly blue mockups.
But once I reach the actual application, there's a considerable drop in perceived sophistication.
The biggest problem is scale.
On a large desktop screen, the application occupies a narrow central strip while enormous areas of white space sit either side. It immediately makes the tool look smaller and less capable than mockups 2 and 4.
Then inside that strip you have lots of relatively small information:
AI Patterns
chart
technical explanation
Hidden Characters
Writing Notes
six checker cards
Known Watermark Check
buttons
The user has to read the interface to understand the conclusion.
Mockup4 does the opposite:
MODERATE AI-PATTERN MATCH
You understand the fundamental answer in about one second.
That's the biggest difference.
Where the live version still beats the mockups
Its branding.
I'd score the Opace identity of the live page around 9/10.
Mockup4 gets fairly close because it retains the Opace header and orange primary action, but your live hero/footer/site integration feels unquestionably like something belonging to opace.co.uk rather than an isolated web application.
So I definitely would not throw away the current visual identity.
I'd effectively combine three things:
Live version: Opace visual personality
Mockup4: core checker UX
Mockup3: broader product architecture
That combination would be substantially better than any individual mockup shown here.
The clearest target in my view is therefore mockup4, but rebuilt in the real Opace design language and eventually expanded with the URL/site/media capabilities illustrated in mockup3.
That could genuinely get into the 9.5/10 range without needing to become visually complicated.
