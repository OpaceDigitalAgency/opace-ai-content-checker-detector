I’m judging these against the actual objective: simple enough for anyone to understand immediately, visually credible, unmistakably Opace, while still exposing enough evidence/science for an advanced user.

1. content-detector-mockup4(1).png — 9.1/10
This is now clearly the strongest design to me.
The big reason is the relationship between evidence and source text.
The visitor doesn't have to mentally connect:
input over here → graph somewhere else → sections somewhere else → technical explanation somewhere else.
Instead they can literally look at what they pasted and see:
this passage = lighter match;
this one = moderate;
this one = stronger;
click it = show score/explanation.
And critically, the original writing still looks like the original writing. There aren't artificial “Section 1” headings inserted into their prose.
That's exactly the interaction I think the live product is missing.
The right-hand result is also considerably stronger visually than the live one. Moderate AI-pattern match dominates first, the ruler provides context, and Text Integrity / Writing Notes remain obviously separate.
Its main weakness is that this screenshot doesn't show all the deeper scientific material you've now built into the live page. But that's easy to add beneath it.
This is the design direction I'd use as the target.
2. content-detector-mockup2(1).png — 8.5/10
This has some of the best visual hierarchy of all five.
The first viewport works very well:
Draft → overall result → scale → integrity → notes.
It's easy to understand where to look.
“Where the pattern appears” is also excellent. The proportional strip communicates the document structure much more effectively than the corresponding visual in the live version.
Where it loses heavily against mockup 4 is this:
Your draft — analysed
It's another copy.
That makes the page longer and makes the user ask:
Why am I looking at my draft twice?
We've already landed on the better answer: annotate the original.
Otherwise this is very strong.
3. Your current live design — 7.8/10
This is where my earlier score changes.
What the live one actually beats the mockups at
It feels most genuinely Opace.
The proper Opace hero, typography, header, footer and warm/dark page structure make this feel like something that genuinely belongs on opace.co.uk rather than a detached SaaS prototype.
And the expanded version contains the best actual information architecture for credibility:
checks performed;
method status;
watermark experiment;
limitations;
methodology;
FAQs;
privacy;
“What this result means”.
There is considerably more real product here than in mockup 1.
But visually it falls behind
Seeing mockup 4 next to it makes that much clearer.
The tool is too physically small
This is probably the biggest purely visual weakness.
On a normal desktop screenshot the important interface occupies a relatively tiny strip down the centre of the page.
The mockups use the available canvas far better.
The live version looks almost like an embedded widget in a marketing page.
I'd increase the working width substantially.
The result doesn't connect tightly enough to its evidence
This is the larger UX issue.
On the live site:
Original draft
then:
AI PATTERNS → No signal → ruler → technical explanation
The user cannot immediately answer:
Which words caused that reading?
Mockup 4 solves that elegantly.
This single change would probably move the live product from 7.8 to ~8.5 by itself.
The live AI panel has too much low-level information competing at once
You currently expose things such as thresholds/model output fairly early.
That information is useful, but it doesn't have enough visual hierarchy.
Compare that with mockup 4:
Moderate AI-pattern match
Huge.
Everything else is subordinate.
The live version feels more like:
Here are the detector's outputs; interpret them.
The ideal feels like:
Here's the answer. Now inspect whatever level of evidence you want.
That's an important difference.
Small typography hurts perceived quality
This is far more obvious beside the mockups.
The live interface has a lot of tiny:
explanatory text;
statuses;
cards;
technical notes;
links.
When viewed at actual page scale, some of it feels compressed rather than refined.
You have enough page space. It doesn't need to be that small.
The six-check expansion is useful but visually busy
I still think the information should exist.
But mockup 4 gives a clue about the better model: summarise first and drill down.
Rather than six roughly similar cards, a compact table/row list would probably communicate:
AI patterns — completed
Hidden characters — clean
Lookalikes — clean
Writing notes — 4
Content credentials — none
Experimental watermark — no public-key match
more efficiently.
4. content-detector-mockup1(1).png — 7.6/10
This one does one thing better than almost everything else:
It looks effortless.
There is almost nothing to learn.
Paste → Check → result.
The 375px and 756px versions also show that the underlying responsive concept is sound.
But now that I've seen what the live product actually does, this design looks too thin.
It hides so much of the capability that the visitor could reasonably think:
another AI detector.
There's no obvious indication that the product is checking hidden characters, provenance, watermark experiments, multiple sections, privacy modes, etc. beyond the very light summaries.
It therefore scores very highly on simplicity, but much lower on authority and differentiation.
And I agree with your earlier criticism of “Some AI signal”. It is markedly weaker than Moderate AI-pattern match.
5. content-detector-mockup3(1).png — 7.2/10
This may actually be the most polished software product visually.
But it's not the best design for this particular job.
It changes the proposition into:
enterprise content-integrity platform.
You immediately get:
persistent sidebar;
history;
website scans;
URL checks;
media scanning;
tables;
review workflows;
audit trail;
dashboard-style evidence.
If Opace eventually develops this into a commercial platform, there are excellent ideas here.
For the free website checker, though, it violates one of your strongest original instincts:
Don't make this feel like technical software.
It has a much steeper perceived learning curve than the others.
So I'm not marking down the craftsmanship. I'm marking down fitness for this product.
The interesting conclusion
If we separate visual/interaction design from actual product substance, the ranking becomes even clearer:
Design	Simplicity	Evidence UX	Credibility	Opace fit
Mockup 4	9.0	9.5	8.5	8.5

Mockup 1	9.5	7.0	6.8	8.2

That explains why I initially liked the live version more than I now score it.
Your live version has the best underlying material. Mockup 4 has the best way of communicating it.
So I would not replace the live site with mockup 4.
I'd effectively combine them:
Live Opace page + live science/content + mockup 4 interaction/result architecture.
If you did that well, I think the finished tool could genuinely get into the 9.2–9.5/10 range rather than merely looking prettier.
