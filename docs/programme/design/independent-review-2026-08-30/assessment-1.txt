1. content-detector-mockup4.png — 9.3/10
This is the one I would build towards.
It solves several things the live version currently struggles with.
The two-column relationship is excellent:
Your draft → Your result
There is almost no cognitive work required to understand the interface.
Then the result immediately says:
Moderate AI-pattern match
followed by a plain-English sentence explaining what happened.
That's significantly better than leading with a technical concept and expecting the user to interpret it.
The visual meter also works unusually well. It isn't pretending that “83% AI” means something absolute. It communicates a spectrum:
No clear match → Light → Moderate → Strong
That's much easier for normal users.
The strongest part
This combination is superb:
highlighted source text
hover/click section result
overall result
visual spectrum
“Where the pattern appears”
simple secondary cards
deeper explanations underneath
It creates a natural information hierarchy:
answer → location → explanation → evidence → methodology
That's basically the product architecture I'd recommend.
Minor issues
The very bottom begins to get slightly busy with six expandable information rows.
And:
Strongest section score: 0.9912
is probably too technical for the default view.
I'd show:
Strongest section: just below Strong
and place 0.9912 inside the explanation panel.
But these are small refinements.

There's also slight duplication between:
Where the pattern appears
and:
Your draft — analysed
although the duplication has a useful purpose.
Best use

5. Current live version — revised to 7.7/10
I don't think it's bad.
Quite the opposite: compared with many real detector tools, it's still respectable.
But these mock-ups show the weaknesses much more clearly.
Its biggest problem isn't visual attractiveness.
It's information architecture.
There are several competing concepts visible immediately:
Paste/upload/example
text
word counts
EU check choice
privacy checks
advanced options
inspect
AI Patterns
graph
hidden characters
writing notes
six checks
watermark
methodology/report controls
Everything is individually reasonable.
Together, it asks users to understand the product rather than simply use it.
The mock-ups reverse that relationship.
One very important difference
Compare the live:
AI PATTERNS
No signal
with mockup4:
Moderate AI-pattern match
Several parts of this draft resemble patterns our AI checker has learnt from AI-generated writing.
The latter is much more effective.
It answers three questions instantly:
What happened?
Moderate match.
What does that mean?
Some passages resemble AI-writing patterns.
Where?
Near the end.
The live version makes the user interpret the graphic and supporting prose.
That's probably the clearest example of why I'd now reduce its score.
