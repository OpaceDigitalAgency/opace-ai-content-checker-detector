04 / Questions

        
What this can, and cannot, tell you

      

      

      

        Why can't you point at the exact sentences?
        

          
The model does not work from a word list, so it cannot point at a phrase. What it mostly notices is
            repetition, or the lack of it. Human writers circle back: they reuse a word from the sentence before
            about three times as often as AI does (6.3% of content words against
            2.1%).

          
We tested that. Make a text repeat itself more like a person does and the score drops
            33 points. Take out every word people associate with AI, every "delve" and
            "robust", and it drops 0.8. The vocabulary is not what gives it away.

          
The rest we cannot name. A simple 24-part scorecard we can read gets
            72.1% of the way to the model's 89.8%. The last
            third is a shape the model learned and cannot explain, and we would rather say that than make
            something up.

          
 Sections are the smallest thing we are entitled to
            point at, which is what the picture above shows.

        Where this check is weakest
        

          
Fiction. 

          
Short pieces.  A short draft weakens a clean
            result more than it weakens a flag.

          
Short web copy.  That is the register most people paste in, and
            we would rather say so than borrow the long-form number.

          
Lightly edited AI. 

        

      


      

        What an AI check can and cannot prove
        

          
A score is a reading of patterns. It is never proof of who wrote something, and we will never present
            it as one. Nobody should lose a mark, a job or a contract on the strength of any detector, ours
            included.

          
The flag point is under review. No level name on this page contains a number, so if the mark moves,
            the words stay true and only the line moves.
