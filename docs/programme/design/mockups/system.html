The Signal Scale, on Opace tokens

One idea, borrowed from something every reader already owns. A phone shows bars. Bars mean how strong the signal is where you are standing. Nobody thinks four bars means the phone approves of them, and every phone user already understands the state that is not a weak signal at all: no service.

02 / The orange

The brand colour stays. The text on it changes.

The first pass replaced Opace orange with a darker substitute. That was the wrong fix, and it was testing the wrong pairing.

What the first pass measured

White text on #FF6A13 reaches 2.87:1 and fails at every size. True — but the live site does not put white text on orange. It puts near-black on it.

The rule, in one line. The brand orange is kept wherever it is a fill, a rule, a dot or a large mark, always with near-black on it. Small orange text uses the site's own darker steps, which is what the live stylesheet already does for eyebrows and FAQ markers. No orange was restyled and no new orange was invented.

03 / Axis A — AI signal

The only axis allowed to say "AI"

Set by the trained model and nothing else. No level name contains a number, so a threshold change cannot make one false.

The document strip

The main result graphic. One block per section, as wide as that section's share of the words and as tall as its reading. The y-axis is the Signal Scale, so the four bands are equal quarters and the flag point is always the same mark. Move the threshold and the bars move under a line that stays still.

Below, the same graphic on a nine-section document. Those readings are not measured — no nine-section run exists anywhere in this repository, so the shape is illustrative and is labelled as such. It is here to show a developer that the layout holds as the section count grows.

The track

Kept for places too small for the strip, and for documents with one section. Same band floors, same rule: the flag point is always the 75 mark, whatever its value.

04 / Axis B and axis C

Different shape, different word, different colour

Read those three questions aloud. They are obviously different questions, so no reader tries to add them up. Three identical meters would be worse than merging the scores, because a reader would average them by eye and get the merged score anyway, without anyone having written it down.

05 / Evidence graphics

Four blocks of prose, replaced

Each of these existed as text in the first pass. The test is whether somebody who reads nothing still gets the right answer.

The accuracy plate

Was a paragraph of statistics in a grey box. The detection rate is a proportion bar, the two false-positive rates are unit grids on the same 100-cell scale, and the worst case is drawn at full height beside the average rather than averaged into it. No competitor in the reference survey publishes a false-positive count with a denominator anywhere a visitor can see it.

The input

Three ways in, all visible at once, no tab row. The paste box is the primary affordance and stays in the first screen; Open a file and Load an example sit in its footer, and the box itself is a drop target with a visible cue. The live tool puts the same three behind a tablist, which hides two of them and costs a 15-word legend to explain.

06 / Icons

At the size they ship

1.5 px strokes, square caps, nothing filled below 2 px. Shown large, then at 16 px.

Route icons

The arrow is the whole message. A reader who sees an arrow leaving their laptop understands the privacy trade in one glance, before any sentence loads. No flag: the region is Belgium and a flag would invite people to read a legal claim into a picture.

07 / Colour and measured contrast

Every ratio on this page was computed in the page

They cannot drift from the tokens, because they are read from them at load.

Axis A uses one hue at increasing intensity, not a red-amber-green ramp. A ramp from good to bad tells the reader that a strong signal is bad and no signal is good, which is a verdict on a person. A single hue that gets stronger says "more", which is what a meter means. Green is reserved for axis B "Clean" and never appears on axis A — green on "No signal" would read as "cleared, human-written", the one claim the engine cannot make.

For comparison. The best-designed free tool in this field, Unmark, fails AA on every status badge it ships: 4.14:1 for DETECTED, 2.86:1 for UNCERTAIN, 2.56:1 for NOT TESTED, all at 12 px. Nothing here is below 4.5:1 for text or 3:1 for a graphic, and no text is set below 13 px.

08 / What must not regress

The build checklist

Press Greyscale test at the top to confirm no state on this page depends on colour.

Every level carries a text label

The icon never appears alone. Fill count and glyph shape carry the meaning without hue.

The meter announces the level and only the level

"AI signal: strong". Never the number — that is announced separately with its own context.

Each block of the document strip is a real button

With its own screen-reader sentence naming the section, its word count, its reading and whether it is the strongest. The chart is not a picture with a caption.

"Not enough text" is announced as not enough text

Never as "no result" and never as an error. Nothing went wrong.

Section focus order follows the document

Even though sections are scored strongest-first internally. A reader is matching the strip against their own draft.

Reduced motion is respected

The bars do not grow into place. A result that animates invites the reader to watch a number climb, and there is no number to climb.

All three ways in stay visible

Paste, a file and an example. Hiding two of them behind a tab row is what the live tool does, and it is why the file input can go missing without anyone noticing.

A check that cannot apply is never drawn as a failure

Its own state, a neutral pill, no location tag, and a sentence saying why.

A run can be stopped

The running state carries a real Stop control. The live tool has one; the first rebuild dropped it.

Nothing interactive under 24 px at 375 px

The live tool has fifteen such targets, including five 19 px toggle rows.

No number may be hard-coded into a label

A build assertion should reject any level label matching a digit, and should check that the top band's floor equals the threshold. They are two separate fields today and nothing stops them drifting apart.

Every published figure carries its denominator and the word "long-form"

A rate without a denominator is the field's commonest dishonesty, and this product's headline figure does not hold for short web copy.

09 / Claim slots

Which strings this page does not own

Press Show claim slots in the black bar to outline every one, with its accessor id. Blue is measured, teal is a boundary of our own check, amber is a fact about somebody else's system and shows the date it was read.

A sentence that asserts something is a slot, filled at runtime and never typed into a page. There are three kinds, and they differ by what can catch them when they go wrong. That is the whole reason the classes exist, so the toggle gives each one its own colour.

measured

A figure we produced. Detection rates, false positives, the corpus line, the flag point. Guarded by its denominator: the accessor refuses a rate that has lost one, and throws rather than printing a figure it cannot source. Filled from measured-figures.ts.

boundary

What our own check can and cannot establish. The six What it says lines on the check cards. Guarded by a predicate over our shipping configuration, checkable at build: it is false only if we change, and we control that. Renders through methodClaim().

Outstanding: the constructor is named and the six lines are tagged, but the predicate is not written. Until it is, these are declared rather than guarded.

world

A fact about somebody else's system. What Anthropic has committed to, what Google's checker accepts, whether any provider has published a key. Nothing we own can guard it. It is false the moment they change and nothing on our side moves to tell us, so it carries a dated review instead — and the toggle prints that date, because an out-of-date world claim is a false claim rather than a slightly old one.

Never add a predicate to a world claim. One that appears to work is guarding a fact about us that happens to correlate with a fact about them, and it will keep passing after the correlation breaks. That is worse than no guard, because it looks guarded.

A world claim is not an unsourced claim. It is sourced to somebody we do not control, which is a different problem and gets a different colour. Painting the two the same would hide the one distinction the class exists to make.

What a world claim does when its review lapses

It degrades. It does not hide, and it does not freeze in the present tense. Printing the age in small type under an unchanged status is the failure being designed against: it keeps asserting a present-tense fact while the disclaimer goes unread, which turns "we do not know" into "we checked".

A slot carries both glosses. The present-tense one and a past-tense one, written by hand, because turning tense mechanically mangles proper nouns and irregular verbs. Where no past gloss exists the fallback is dated attribution — clumsier, but never ungrammatical and never asserting the present tense.

The evidence survives and keeps its date. Only the present tense is removed, because that is the only part that has actually expired. Backstop: at review date + 30 days the build fails with [[STALE: …]], the same visible-failure token as [[MISSING: …]]. The degraded state is honest for a few weeks; it is not a place to live. All of it is date arithmetic at build time against each slot's verifiedAt and reviewDays — no cron, nothing a person can forget.

The provider status panel is where this class was defined and where most of it lives: nine of its twelve claim-bearing lines are world claims. It is specified in PROVIDER-STATUS-PANEL-2026-08-29.md and built as provider-status-panel.html, both by another session. It is not part of these five files and none of its twelve slots is among the ones below.

10 / What was deleted

And why it is not coming back

Each of these was on the live tool or in the first pass.

The headline percentage

"80.8%" reads as large to a human being whatever word sits beside it, and produces two separate misreadings: that most of the document is AI, and that the reading is nearly certain. Words cannot outrun a number in a glance. Replaced by a level name and the document strip, with the raw figure kept in the detail line beside its flag point.

The "Highlights in your draft" panel

It carried AI evidence, editing advice and rewrite anchors on one surface and needed two disclaimers to say what it was not. A panel that has to tell you twice what it is not is the wrong panel.

Sentence-level highlighting on axis A

Only about a third of sentences push a document towards machine, so two highlights in three would be wrong, and a reader reads a highlight as an accusation every time. Sections are the smallest unit the tool is entitled to point at.

A single Integrity Score out of 100

The obvious answer, the one every competitor ships, and the one this architecture exists to prevent. It would let a zero-width space that any paste handler can insert drag an authorship score upwards.

The other two naming schemes

The Signal Scale is the decision. The live ladder and "Flagged / Not flagged" now survive only on compare.html, as the record of why.

The invented orange

The first pass substituted #C2410C for the brand colour. Section 02 above shows why that was unnecessary.
